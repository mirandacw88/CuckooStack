The level editor

Cuckoo Stack’s campaign is 30 hand-tuned levels, and every other level from 11 plays a maze gauntlet. The level editor is a web app on your own machine for designing them: a level’s whole course, every hazard on it, which gauntlet it plays and where, and each gauntlet’s grid cell by cell. The game’s own checkers judge every edit as you make it, and the game’s bot plays each level a moment after you stop. What the editor calls Ready to ship is exactly what the game’s tests will check before a commit.

Run it

python3 tools/level_editor/server.py --open

It builds the checkers and the desktop game first (cmake --build build), then opens http://127.0.0.1:8765. It listens on this machine only. --no-build skips the build; --port picks another port.

Where things are saved

Levels: assets/levels/level_NN.txt. Gauntlets: assets/gauntlets/<name>.txt. Saving also rebuilds the game’s two layouts files (src/core/LevelLayouts.inc, GauntletLayouts.inc). Rebuild the game to ship them; Play plays your edits straight away, saved or not.

The workflow

  1. Edit a level’s course in the Levels view, or a gauntlet’s grid in the Gauntlets view.
  2. Check. The inspector’s Ready to ship card lists every check the tests make; red items block commits.
  3. Play it in the desktop game, or Watch the bot play it, from a start point near your edit.
  4. Save (⌘S): every unsaved edit, together, into the game’s files. The History shows what you’d save, or revert.
  5. Rebuild and commit. The pre-commit hook runs every test; a level or gauntlet that isn’t ready blocks the commit.

Words used here

Cell
The grid’s unit: 0.6 m along, and one egg high. Walls, saws and gauntlets all line up to cells.
Row
A height in cells (eggs). Row 0 is the street.
Wall
A run of columns of the same height and kind (crates, hay, or hay-topped crates). She climbs one if her egg stack is as high as it.
Barrier
A girder over a wall: her head must pass under it, so it caps her stack.
Saw
A blade set in a wall top: each crossing saws off her bottom egg; with no egg under her, it gets her.
Disco ball
A pickup: +5 points and an egg and a half for her meter.
Gauntlet
A maze laid into a level’s course: she runs it on her own at full speed, turned back by bumpers and gates. The course brings her to its door on a forecourt, under a lintel as low as the door, and takes her on from a porch.
Egg meter
The eggs she can lay, refilling as she runs. The checker’s egg budget is what a careful player holds along the course.
Near misses
PERFECT (a climb with no egg to spare), CLOSE SHAVE (a blade takes her last egg), CLOSE CALL (a squeeze under a roof, her head brushing it).
The bot
The game’s own player: it lays an egg only when it needs one. The editor runs it headless through every level to check it can be cleared.
Start point
Where Play and Watch the bot start, so you can test an edit without playing from the beginning.

The editor at a glance

Two views share one top bar. Levels edits a level’s whole course; Gauntlets edits a maze’s grid. ⇧G switches between a level and its gauntlet. Click a number to jump to its section.

Hover anything in the editor for a moment and a card says what it does (and its key). Every card’s text is in static/js/tips.js; this wiki shows the same text.

The top bar

The same in both views: the view switch, where you are (a level or a gauntlet), and the actions. Hover these buttons: they show the editor’s own tooltips.

Tip. The unsaved count is everything not yet saved, in both views. Save saves all of it together, because some edits belong together (a gauntlet moved from one level to another changes both).

Levels view

A campaign level’s whole course, side on, as the game lays it, in its zone’s look (the neon city, Neon Egypt, Neon Japan). Everything in the level file is editable here: its walls, barriers, saws, disco balls, finish line, difficulty, the droid’s raids, its description, and which maze gauntlet it plays.

The level picker

The number in the top bar opens it (or press L): every level by zone, each with its state (green ready, red not ready, pulsing still checking), a miniature of its course, its gauntlet, and a dot if it has unsaved edits. ⌥← and ⌥→ go to the level before and after. The editor checks every level in the background, so the picker’s dots are the whole campaign’s state.

The course

What the canvas shows, left to right along the course (a cell is 0.6 m; the ruler at the top is in metres, the one at the left in rows):

  • Scroll to look along it; ⌘-scroll or pinch zooms at the pointer; Space-drag pans. F fits the height; [ ] zoom.
  • The minimap above it is the whole course; click or drag it to look along.
  • The line at the bottom left says where the pointer is: metres, column, row, and what’s there.
  • Hover a hazard (a saw, a barrier, a disco ball, the gauntlet, a note, the finish, the start) and a card says what it is and does there:

Its tools

The tools column on the left. A tool’s key picks it; ⌥ or a right click with most tools takes away instead (down to the street, barriers off, a saw removed).

Gauntlet columns are the grid’s. Walls, saws and column edits stop at a gauntlet’s span: edit its grid instead (double-click it). A level plays one gauntlet at most, and a gauntlet plays in one level at most.

Wall kinds

The swatches at the top of the tools column (keys 1 2 3). The kind picked is what new walls are made of, and what the Kind brush paints.

Overlays

The chips at the top right of the course turn layers on and off.

The inspector

The cards on the right, top to bottom.

Ready to ship

Every check the game’s tests make on a level, item by item (which test checks what). Green: it passes. Red: the tests fail, and block commits, until it does. Orange: a warning (the egg budget running short, a note) that no test fails on, but worth a look. Click an item with a place (a crash, a note, a door) to see it on the course; its gauntlet’s item opens the gauntlet.

History

Every edit to this level, newest first: what it did in words (“Saw added at 92.1 m”) and how long ago. The one you’re at is highlighted; edits you’ve undone are dimmed (click one to make it again); SAVED marks what’s saved. Click an edit to go back to just after it. Hover one and its spot pulses on the course; its target button looks there without going back. The heading says what’s changed since the save. More in Saving, history, revert.

The level

Under Difficulty, the speed and egg refill the band gives, from the level’s start to its finish (from Neon Egypt on, the curve is flat at 12.4 m/s and an egg every 0.40 s). The zone is set by the level’s number.

In the game, the course, notes, description

In the game: the bot’s headless run, cleared in how many seconds with how many near misses, or where it crashed (“show me”). The course: its numbers (walls, barriers, the tallest wall, saws and disco balls, the least eggs a careful player holds). Notes: where the checker finds it unfair, each clickable. Description: the notes at the top of the level’s file.

The bot’s run, replayed

The footer replays the bot’s run in the game along the course: the hen and her egg stack, at 0.5× to 4×. The track is the run metre by metre: the eggs a careful player holds (the checker’s egg budget, cyan), the bot’s stack (volt), its near misses (dots), and where it crashed (red). Click or drag the track to look. P plays, , . step, Home End go to its ends.

Gauntlets view

A maze gauntlet’s grid, cell by cell, as the level laying it plays it. The game’s own solver checks every edit (one way through, no traps, the egg meter, near misses), and the game’s bot plays it a moment later.

Tabs, new gauntlets

One tab a gauntlet, by the level it’s in (“NO LEVEL” if none lays it yet), with its state and an unsaved dot. New gauntlet asks for a name (lower case letters, digits, _: it names its file) and starts it as a plain room with a door each side. A new gauntlet is in no level until a level’s Gauntlet setting puts it in one; never saved, Revert throws it away.

Cells

The swatches: the first cell (volt) paints with a click, the second (cyan, dashed) with a right click; X swaps them. Keys 1–0.

The rules a grid keeps (the game’s parser): a door on each edge, the entry on the left; the top row solid; each door on a floor with room for her (2 cells); one entry, one exit. A grid that breaks one doesn’t parse, and is never saved.

Its tools

Overlays

Checks, signatures, near misses

Its Ready to ship card lists: a valid grid; out by stacking alone; one way through (every cell on it unavoidable); no traps (a dead end must end her run, never bounce her forever); out with the meter at its hardest (refilling 1.25× slower, 2 eggs at the door); 15 or more near misses; unlike every other gauntlet (mirror images count); its design’s signature (each Egypt gauntlet is built round one: the Grand Gallery’s 15 one-egg steps, the wells, the labyrinth’s forks, the ascent’s passage; src/core/GauntletDesigns.cpp); on its level’s course; and the game’s bot getting out, showing the solver’s near misses.

The replay

The solver’s bot through the grid, move by move, with the egg meter at the level’s refill or 1.25× or 1.4× slower: the hen and her stack on the grid, the meter and her stack over time on the track.

Pieces and hazards

What each piece of a course does in the game, as the course view draws it. Hover them in the editor for these cards.

Checks and the tests

The pre-commit hook and the build’s CI run every test; a failure blocks the commit. These are the tests that hold every level and gauntlet to a standard, and the Ready to ship items that show them first.

Tests of how the game plays (the tomb’s tunnel, a saw’s cut, the camera in a maze) run on frozen copies of the levels and gauntlets (tests/fixtures), so tuning content never breaks them. Only the tests above look at the levels you edit.

Saving, history, revert

Save

Save (⌘S) saves every unsaved level and grid together, each checked as the game will read it. Nothing is written if any of it doesn’t parse, or if a gauntlet would be in two levels. A level whose gauntlet’s grid grew or shrank is refitted (what’s after its span moves on or back). Saving writes the files and both layouts files; if something fails its checks it asks first, since the tests will block commits until it passes.

History

Every level and every grid keeps its own history: each edit described in words, when, and where. Undo and Redo step along it (their tooltips name the step); the History card shows all of it and jumps anywhere in it. Reverting is an edit of its own (“Back to the save”), so what came before it stays to go back to. Histories are kept in this browser across reloads, beside your unsaved drafts.

Changes

The Changes overlay shows what’s changed since the save on the canvas: the saved course as a dashed ghost, columns put in outlined green, columns taken out marked “−N”, saws and disco balls added ringed green and removed ones ghosted red, the saved finish line as a ghost flag. In a grid, changed cells are outlined, and hovering one says what it was.

Revert

Revert, or clicking the unsaved count, lists every unsaved edit with the ones on screen ticked (in the Levels view, the level and the gauntlet it lays). Each says what’s changed and expands to its edits since the save. Revert takes the ticked ones back to what’s saved; Show opens one with Changes on; Save all saves them.

Testing a level

Play

Opens the desktop game on the level as edited, saved or not: you play (tap or Space lays an egg). In the Gauntlets view, every run starts at the gauntlet’s door unless it has a start point.

Watch the bot

The desktop game, the game’s bot playing the level as edited.

Start points

To test an edit without playing from the beginning, set a start point with the Start tool (T), in either view. Play and Watch the bot then start every run there, and a chip in the canvas’s corner says where (✕ clears it).

While you test from a start point, clearing the level plays it again from there rather than moving on to the next. Start points are a testing aid kept in your browser, not part of the level. An edit that leaves one in a wall or a span moves it to the nearest good spot, or clears it.

The Simulator

Simulator builds the iOS app with what’s saved and installs it on every running iOS Simulator (a few minutes; it offers to save first). It starts at the level’s beginning: in the game, Developer menu → Course level, then Jump to gauntlet.

Changing the editor

For whoever extends the editor next: how it’s built, how to add to it, and the one rule.

Its parts

FileWhat it is
tools/level_editor/server.pyThe local server: serves the app and this wiki, runs the checkers (build/level_check, build/gauntlet_check), saves files, opens the desktop game, builds for the Simulator. Python’s standard library only.
static/js/app.jsThe shell: the two views, the top bar, Save, Revert, the keyboard.
static/js/levels.js, courseview.js, course.jsThe Levels view; its canvas; the course model (a level file read into columns, every edit).

static/js/store.js, dialog.js, util.jsWhat both views share; the in-page dialogs; small helpers.

Recipes

Add a tool

  1. Its icon, key and label in tools.js; its tip in tips.js.
  2. What it does on the canvas in levels.js (or gauntlets.js): its pointerdown case, and an edit through commit(L, kind).
  3. Its words in the History: a kind case in describe.js.
  4. Its model in course.js / grid.js, tested in tests/*.test.mjs.
  5. Its section here, with a figure, and a changelog line.

Add a hazard

  1. The game first: its code, its place in the level file (LevelData.h) and the checker (level_check.cpp).
  2. The course model (course.js: read, write, edit) and its drawing (courseview.js).
  3. A tool for it, its hover card (hazardAt in levels.js) and its tip.
  4. Its card in this wiki’s Pieces and hazards.

Add a check

  1. The test that enforces it, in the game’s tests.
  2. The checker reporting it in its JSON.
  3. An item in the view’s verdict(), so Ready to ship says what the test will.
  4. A row in Checks and the tests.

Its tests

ctest runs them with the game’s: level_editor (the server: serving, checking, saving, playing, what it turns away) and level_editor_js (in Node: the course and grid models, the history and its words, and this wiki, which must have a section for every tip).

The rule. Every change to the editor (a tool, hazard, check, panel, button, shortcut, file format) updates this wiki in the same change: its section, its figure, its tip in tips.js, and a line in the changelog. The level_editor_js test fails when a tip has no section here (id="tip-<key>"), so an undocumented feature blocks commits.
