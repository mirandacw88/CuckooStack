The level editor

Cuckoo Stack’s campaign is 30 hand-tuned levels, and every other level from 11 plays a maze gauntlet. The level editor is a web app on your own machine for designing them: a level’s whole course, every hazard on it, which gauntlet it plays and where, and each gauntlet’s grid cell by cell. The game’s own checkers judge every edit as you make it, and the game’s bot plays each level a moment after you stop. What the editor calls Ready to ship is exactly what the game’s tests will check before a commit.

Run it

python3 tools/level_editor/server.py --open

It builds the checkers and the desktop game first (cmake --build build), then opens http://127.0.0.1:8765. It listens on this machine only. --no-build skips the build; --port picks another port.

Where things are saved

Levels: assets/levels/level_NN.txt. Gauntlets: assets/gauntlets/<name>.txt. Saving also rebuilds the game’s two layouts files (src/core/LevelLayouts.inc, GauntletLayouts.inc). Rebuild the game to ship them; Play plays your edits straight away, saved or not.

The workflow

  1. Edit a level’s course in the Levels view, or a gauntlet’s grid in the Gauntlets view.
  2. Check. The inspector’s Ready to ship card lists every check the tests make; red items block commits.
  3. Play it in the desktop game, or Watch the bot play it, from a start point near your edit.
  4. Save (⌘S): every unsaved edit, together, into the game’s files. The History shows what you’d save, or revert.
  5. Rebuild and commit. The pre-commit hook runs every test; a level or gauntlet that isn’t ready blocks the commit.

Words used here

Cell
The grid’s unit: 0.6 m along, and one egg high. Walls, saws and gauntlets all line up to cells.
Row
A height in cells (eggs). Row 0 is the street.
Wall
A run of columns of the same height and kind (crates, hay, or hay-topped crates). She climbs one if her egg stack is as high as it.
Barrier
A girder over a wall: her head must pass under it, so it caps her stack.
Saw
A blade set in a wall top: each crossing saws off her bottom egg; with no egg under her, it gets her.
Disco ball
A pickup: +5 points and an egg and a half for her meter.
Gauntlet
A maze laid into a level’s course: she runs it on her own at full speed, turned back by bumpers and gates. The course brings her to its door on a forecourt, under a lintel as low as the door, and takes her on from a porch.
Egg meter
The eggs she can lay, refilling as she runs. The checker’s egg budget is what a careful player holds along the course.
Near misses
PERFECT (a climb with no egg to spare), CLOSE SHAVE (a blade takes her last egg), CLOSE CALL (a squeeze under a roof, her head brushing it).
The bot
The game’s own player: it lays an egg only when it needs one. The editor runs it headless through every level to check it can be cleared.
Start point
Where Play and Watch the bot start, so you can test an edit without playing from the beginning.

The editor at a glance

Two views share one top bar. Levels edits a level’s whole course; Gauntlets edits a maze’s grid. ⇧G switches between a level and its gauntlet. Click a number to jump to its section.

Hover anything in the editor for a moment and a card says what it does (and its key). Every card’s text is in static/js/tips.js; this wiki shows the same text.

The top bar

The same in both views: the view switch, where you are (a level or a gauntlet), and the actions. Hover these buttons: they show the editor’s own tooltips.

Tip. The unsaved count is everything not yet saved, in both views. Save saves all of it together, because some edits belong together (a gauntlet moved from one level to another changes both).

Levels view

A campaign level’s whole course, side on, as the game lays it, in its zone’s look (the neon city, Neon Egypt, Neon Japan). Everything in the level file is editable here: its walls, barriers, saws, disco balls, finish line, difficulty, the droid’s raids, its description, and which maze gauntlet it plays.

The level picker

The number in the top bar opens it (or press L): every level by zone, each with its state (green ready, red not ready, pulsing still checking), a miniature of its course, its gauntlet, and a dot if it has unsaved edits. ⌥← and ⌥→ go to the level before and after. The editor checks every level in the background, so the picker’s dots are the whole campaign’s state.

The course

What the canvas shows, left to right along the course (a cell is 0.6 m; the ruler at the top is in metres, the one at the left in rows):

  • Scroll to look along it; ⌘-scroll or pinch zooms at the pointer; Space-drag pans. F fits the height; [ ] zoom.
  • The minimap above it is the whole course; click or drag it to look along.
  • The line at the bottom left says where the pointer is: metres, column, row, and what’s there.
  • Hover a hazard (a saw, a barrier, a disco ball, a laser, lava, a spring, the gauntlet, a note, the finish, the start) and a card says what it is and does there:

Its tools

The tools column on the left. A tool’s key picks it; ⌥ or a right click with most tools takes away instead (down to the street, barriers off, a saw removed).

Bouncers

The tools column’s Bouncers group: the spring, and the bouncer walls. Bumper (U), Gate → (Y) and Gate ← (⇧Y) work like the Walls tool, their walls of their own kind: drag across columns, each as high as the pointer, 1 at least (⇧: as high as where you started); ⌥ or right drag: down to the street. A bouncer wall is never the course’s first wall (she comes to it), and never has a barrier or floating block over it: built over one, it takes it off; the Barrier and Block tools leave bouncers alone, and columns aren’t taken out or put in so a bouncer ends up first. In the level file they’re walls like any: 2 4 bumper, 1 3 gate>, 1 3 gate<.

In the game, each group of bouncer walls (40 cells apart at most) is a bounce zone, played by a maze’s rules: she bounces off bumpers and gates set against her, back and forth, and stacks while she bounces to climb out over the last one. The course shades each zone faintly, from the column she comes to it in to its last bouncer, with a bounce zone tag (hover it for its span). The checker judges each zone with the maze’s solver: no way over its last bouncer, a bumper with no way out, an egg budget that runs short in it, and and a saw or lava on the street or a wall laser in it (not yet) are its notes. A spring on a zone’s wall top is a spring cell there: the zone’s solver flies her the maze’s arc.

Gauntlet columns are the grid’s. Walls, saws and column edits stop at a gauntlet’s span: edit its grid instead (double-click it). A level plays one gauntlet at most, and a gauntlet plays in one level at most.

Wall kinds

The swatches at the top of the tools column (keys 1 2 3). The kind picked is what new walls are made of, and what the Kind brush paints.

Bumpers and one-way gates are wall kinds too (bumper, gate>, gate<), built with their own tools: see Bouncers. The Kind brush paints a bouncer back to the kind picked.

Overlays

The chips at the top right of the course turn layers on and off.

The inspector

The cards on the right, top to bottom.

Ready to ship

Every check the game’s tests make on a level, item by item (which test checks what). Green: it passes. Red: the tests fail, and block commits, until it does. Orange: a warning (the egg budget running short, a note) that no test fails on, but worth a look. Click an item with a place (a crash, a note, a door) to see it on the course; its gauntlet’s item opens the gauntlet.

History

Every edit to this level, newest first: what it did in words (“Saw added at 92.1 m”) and how long ago. The one you’re at is highlighted; edits you’ve undone are dimmed (click one to make it again); SAVED marks what’s saved. Click an edit to go back to just after it. Hover one and its spot pulses on the course; its target button looks there without going back. The heading says what’s changed since the save. More in Saving, history, revert.

The level

Under Difficulty, the speed and egg refill the band gives, from the level’s start to its finish (from Neon Egypt on, the curve is flat at 12.4 m/s and an egg every 0.40 s). The zone is set by the level’s number.

In the game, the course, notes, description

In the game: the bot’s headless run, cleared in how many seconds with how many near misses, or where it crashed (“show me”). The course: its numbers (walls, barriers, the tallest wall, saws and disco balls, the least eggs a careful player holds). Notes: where the checker finds it unfair, each clickable. Description: the notes at the top of the level’s file.

The bot’s run, replayed

The footer replays the bot’s run in the game along the course: the hen and her egg stack, at 0.5× to 4×. The track is the run metre by metre: the eggs a careful player holds (the checker’s egg budget, cyan), the bot’s stack (volt), its near misses (dots), and where it crashed (red). Click or drag the track to look. P plays, , . step, Home End go to its ends.

Gauntlets view

A maze gauntlet’s grid, cell by cell, as the level laying it plays it. The game’s own solver checks every edit (one way through, no traps, the egg meter, near misses), and the game’s bot plays it a moment later.

Tabs, new gauntlets

One tab a gauntlet, by the level it’s in (“NO LEVEL” if none lays it yet), with its state and an unsaved dot. New gauntlet asks for a name (lower case letters, digits, _: it names its file) and starts it as a plain room with a door each side. A new gauntlet is in no level until a level’s Gauntlet setting puts it in one; never saved, Revert throws it away.

Rename (by its name, over the checks) gives it a new name: its file moves (assets/gauntlets/<new>.txt), the level that lays it says the new name (nothing else in it changes, its history too), and the game’s banner as she enters shows it with spaces (“THE HALL OF SAWS”). One that’s saved is renamed on disk at once; a new one, when it’s saved. On the iPhone and iPad it’s the name field in the maze’s card; the Mac is told at the next sync.

Cells

The swatches: the first cell (volt) paints with a click, the second (cyan, dashed) with a right click; X swaps them. Keys 1–0, then [ and ] (a wall laser firing left, right) = (lava) and \ (a spring); - and + zoom.

The spring

A spring (^) is a floor like stone, with the course spring’s pad on it (a coiled lime plate that bobs). She is launched as her middle reaches the cell’s middle: up 5 rows and on at 8 m/s, whatever the run’s speed, with no eggs laid in the air. The solver flies her the same arc the game does, so the replay jumps from the spring to where she lands; the bot’s way draws it as a dotted lime arc ending in a dot. She comes down on the first floor or ledge under her; a bumper or gate against her bounces her mid-air; a wall, a roof or a ledge’s underside in her way ends her run, and so do a blade within a row of her path, a laser’s beam, lava, a spring or the exit under her landing (put the landing in a clear stretch, and the spring clear of the hazards). Springs make a maze’s floating ledges reachable from the floor below, and let a room fly her up into the one above instead of the stacked wall. A course spring on a bounce zone’s wall top is a spring cell there too.

The rules a grid keeps (the game’s parser): a door on each edge, the entry on the left; the top row solid; each door on a floor with room for her (2 cells); one entry, one exit. A grid that breaks one doesn’t parse, and is never saved.

Its tools

Overlays

Checks, signatures, near misses

Its Ready to ship card lists: a valid grid; out by stacking alone; one way through (every cell on it unavoidable); no traps (a dead end must end her run, never bounce her forever); out with the meter at its hardest (refilling 1.25× slower, 2 eggs at the door); 15 or more near misses; unlike every other gauntlet (mirror images count); its design’s signature (each Egypt gauntlet is built round one: the Grand Gallery’s 15 one-egg steps, the wells, the labyrinth’s forks, the ascent’s passage; src/core/GauntletDesigns.cpp); on its level’s course; and the game’s bot getting out, showing the solver’s near misses.

The replay

The solver’s bot through the grid, move by move, with the egg meter at the level’s refill or 1.25× or 1.4× slower: the hen and her stack on the grid, the meter and her stack over time on the track.

Pieces and hazards

What each piece of a course does in the game, as the course view draws it. Hover them in the editor for these cards.

Checks and the tests

The pre-commit hook and the build’s CI run every test; a failure blocks the commit. These are the tests that hold every level and gauntlet to a standard, and the Ready to ship items that show them first.

Tests of how the game plays (the tomb’s tunnel, a saw’s cut, the camera in a maze) run on frozen copies of the levels and gauntlets (tests/fixtures), so tuning content never breaks them. Only the tests above look at the levels you edit.

Saving, history, revert

Save

Save (⌘S) saves every unsaved level and grid together, each checked as the game will read it. Nothing is written if any of it doesn’t parse, or if a gauntlet would be in two levels. A level whose gauntlet’s grid grew or shrank is refitted (what’s after its span moves on or back). Saving writes the files and both layouts files; if something fails its checks it asks first, since the tests will block commits until it passes.

History

Every level and every grid keeps its own history: each edit described in words, when, and where. Undo and Redo step along it (their tooltips name the step); the History card shows all of it and jumps anywhere in it. Reverting is an edit of its own (“Back to the save”), so what came before it stays to go back to. Histories are kept in this browser across reloads, beside your unsaved drafts.

Changes

The Changes overlay shows what’s changed since the save on the canvas: the saved course as a dashed ghost, columns put in outlined green, columns taken out marked “−N”, saws and disco balls added ringed green and removed ones ghosted red, the saved finish line as a ghost flag. In a grid, changed cells are outlined, and hovering one says what it was.

Revert

Revert, or clicking the unsaved count, lists every unsaved edit with the ones on screen ticked (in the Levels view, the level and the gauntlet it lays). Each says what’s changed and expands to its edits since the save. Revert takes the ticked ones back to what’s saved; Show opens one with Changes on; Save all saves them.

Testing a level

Play

Opens the desktop game on the level as edited, saved or not: you play (tap or Space lays an egg). In the Gauntlets view, every run starts at the gauntlet’s door unless it has a start point.

Watch the bot

The desktop game, the game’s bot playing the level as edited.

Start points

To test an edit without playing from the beginning, set a start point with the Start tool (T), in either view. Play and Watch the bot then start every run there, and a chip in the canvas’s corner says where (✕ clears it).

While you test from a start point, clearing the level plays it again from there rather than moving on to the next. Start points are a testing aid kept in your browser, not part of the level. An edit that leaves one in a wall or a span moves it to the nearest good spot, or clears it.

The Simulator

Simulator builds the iOS app with what’s saved and installs it on every running iOS Simulator (a few minutes; it offers to save first). It starts at the level’s beginning: in the game, Developer menu → Course level, then Jump to gauntlet.

Chunks: your own set pieces

The generators’ creativity is limited by their grammar; the cleverest pieces in the game are the ones built by hand. A chunk is a stretch of course you build once in the iPhone and iPad editor and the generator places in the courses it rolls: Roll a new course may lay it, flipped or not, as one of its pieces. A maze chunk is a block of cells that Generate a new maze stamps into a floor. Chunks sync to the Mac like levels and mazes, and are baked into the game’s own generated levels from 31: see Sync and the game below.)

The start screen

The iPad and iPhone editor opens on two buttons: Edit Levels (the levels list beside the editor, as ever) and Edit Chunks. The Home button (⌃⌘H; the house, top left of each list, and on the iPad in the editor’s bar) comes back. Launch arguments: --open N goes to a level, --chunks [id] to the chunks (that one open).

The chunk list

In bands of five levels (1–5, 6–10, 11–15 …) by where each chunk’s first range starts, then by that start and end. A row: its name (the first line of its description, else its file’s name), a course or maze icon, a chip for every range, its weight, its uses (“2 a level” or “No limit”), the flip mark, and the bot’s verdict. New course chunk (⌘N) asks for a name and starts you with a run-in, a wall and a run-out; swipe left to take one out (it asks first), swipe right or hold for Copy. New maze chunk asks for a name too and starts you with a 9 × 5 block (a saw in the floor).

Levels it may appear in

A chunk has one or more ranges of consecutive levels (1–5, 6–10, a single 7): a from and a to each, typed or stepped, ranges may overlap. A chunk appears only when you tap Roll a new course on a level one of its ranges covers; your saved levels 1–30 never change unless you roll them. The chunk is judged at the level its first range starts at (its zone, its difficulty, its bot).

Weight and uses

Weight (1 to 10): how much the generator wants it; a plain piece weighs 1. Max uses a level: the most one course holds, or No limit.

Mirror

With Mirror on, the generator may flip the chunk left to right (a laser fires the other way, climbs become drops). It is off, with the reason, when the chunk holds a spring (they only launch rightwards) or a bumper or gate. The checker and the bot judge the flipped chunk too: a note that starts “Flipped:” says what is unfair that way (at the place it is in the chunk as built), and the bot card plays it flipped and says if it crashes; a chunk that is only fair one way is better with Mirror off.

The chunk editor

The same canvas and tools as a level’s (no Gauntlet tool), the pads drawn hatched, the finish line shorter (12 m at the least). The inspector: Chunk settings (its name, ranges, weight, uses, Mirror, description), the checker (a note in red for a hazard in a pad), the bot (it plays the chunk alone), the history (undo, and a step for every edit; the settings are saved at once and are not steps). Play and Watch play the chunk on its own.

Where you see them used

After Roll a new course a notice says which chunks it placed (“hurdles ×2, steps”), the roll’s history step names them, and each is outlined on the canvas with its name (⇄ when flipped) until you edit the course. A chunk whose file does not read the way the generator needs (a hazard in a pad, say) is left out of the roll, and the notice says so; its row in the list says why.

A roll never comes back empty

A chunk that no course or maze will hold (a demanding one: three saws in five cells) never costs you the roll. Roll a new course keeps the density you asked for and lays the course without the chunk that spoils it (with several chunks it tries leaving out each alone first, so the good ones stay); Generate a new maze tries a dozen times with the chunks, then a few with fewer (half the weight, one use each), then without them. The notice says so (“Left out: three-saws — no maze passed with it at level 20” or “Fewer of: …”), and the roll’s history step reads “left out: …”. A maze with chunks takes about a second or two to come back, not ten.

A maze chunk

A block of the maze’s own cells, top row first, with no doors, as small as 3 × 3: you build it with the maze editor’s tools and cells (no Doors, no Start, no Play). Its bottom row is the floor she runs on (stone, with a saw, lava or a spring set into it as in a maze); the rows above it are the air she runs through; stone in its top row reaches up to the roof where it is placed. The generator stamps it into a stretch of a floor that has the head room and the length for it, where there is no ledge or spring pad, hazards either side with a gap the egg meter refills in. The inspector holds the same Chunk settings as a course chunk’s (ranges, weight, max uses a maze, Mirror), the checker, the egg meter and the history.

Mirror, for a maze

A maze floor that runs left is the floor run right with every column reversed, so a maze chunk is drawn as she runs through it going right: on a floor that runs left the tower draws it mirrored, and she still runs through it as you built it. With Mirror on the generator may also flip it as she runs, and puts it on floors that run either way; with Mirror off it goes only on floors that run right, as drawn. A chunk with a spring cannot be flipped (a spring launches her the way she runs), so its Mirror switch is off, with the reason, and it goes on floors that run right only.

Backups

The chunk list’s Backup button. Back up chunks… saves one master file, Cuckoo Chunks <date>.cuckoochunks (JSON: its format and version, the date, the app’s version and every chunk’s id and whole file), wherever you pick in Files. Restore chunks… picks one, shows what it would do (new, changed, unchanged chunks, and those only here, which stay) and, when you confirm, does it as one step you can undo (the banner’s Undo the restore); the chunks it replaces are first saved in a chunks_before_restore file in the app’s folder. The levels’ own Back up all levels… file holds the chunks too (a separate chunks key; older backups without it restore as before). Syncing to the Mac writes one file per chunk, assets/chunks/<id>.txt (below).

Sync and the game

Chunks sync with the Mac in the same Sync now as levels and mazes (the banner is at the top of the chunk list too). A chunk changed on the Mac only is pulled; changed here only, pushed (the Mac takes it only if its file is still the one you edited from, and only once level_check --chunk reads it, and for a maze chunk plays it in the test corridor, flipped too with Mirror on: a chunk that breaks the rules or that she can’t get through is turned away by name, with the reason, and the others still go); taken out here, moved to build/editor-trash on the Mac. A chunk changed on both sides is a choice, as a maze is: keep mine, take the Mac’s, or keep mine as a copy (-copy) and take the Mac’s. A chunk renamed here is, to the Mac, one taken out and one new. A row with an amber arrow is not synced yet. The first launch on a fresh device starts with the chunks the app was built with (the repo’s assets/chunks), already agreed with the Mac. On the Mac every chunk push builds src/core/ChunkLayouts.inc (scripts/chunks/build_chunks.py, which never touches levels or gauntlets), and the game’s generated levels, 31 and up, place the chunks whose ranges cover them; the saved levels 1–30 are never changed.

The iPhone and iPad editor

Cuckoo Editor (the CuckooEditor app: src/platform/ios_editor) edits the same level files on an iPhone, an iPad mini or any iPad, upright or on its side, with no network. It runs the game’s own code: the course model, the checker and its notes, the egg meter, the bot in the background after every edit (its verdict on every level in the list), and the real game to play-test in (by hand, or the bot playing, up to 4× fast, from the level’s start or a start flag), so what it says is what the tests and the game will. Its hazard tools are the game’s registry’s (Hazards.h): a new hazard brings its tool, its numbers and its look with it.

iPhone

The canvas edge to edge, the tools along the bottom (on its side: a rail at the side), the inspector a sheet from the bottom, play full screen. One finger uses the tool; two pan and pinch.

iPad and iPad mini

The tool rail, the whole course in a minimap over the canvas, the inspector a column on the right. Play and Watch open over the editor in an iPhone’s frame, the game as big and laid out as on an iPhone (the real perspective). A hardware keyboard: the tools’ keys, ⌘Z, ⌥ to erase, Space to play.

Pan, history, revert

The Pan tool (the hand, H) drags the course along with one finger or the Pencil, and lets it glide; two fingers pan with any tool. Hold the Start tool for its menu: back to the level’s start (clears the flag; ⇧T on a keyboard), show the flag, or place one. Each level’s History (the clock) lists every edit in words, when and where, kept on the device between launches: tap one to go back to just after it, SYNCED marks where it last matched the Mac’s. Its revert goes back to the synced version or the game’s original, as a step of its own (undo takes it back); a level sync updates is a step too.

The maze editor

On a level with a maze gauntlet, the Gauntlet tool (G) opens its grid in place of the course; Done (Esc) goes back. Its rail: Pan, Brush (a cell, or a stroke), Box, Doors (a row: the nearer door moved there), Columns and Rows (drag: copied in; with the eraser, or held: taken out), Start (the play-test from a cell); the ten cells under them (keys 1–0; the first paints, the second is the eraser's; X swaps). Every edit must read as the game reads a grid, or it's turned away with why. The game's checker judges it a moment after each edit: the bot's way through, where she gets (Reach), the near misses, the egg meter at 1, 1.25 and 1.4× slower, then the game's bot playing it; the checklist is what the tests want (tap an item to see where). A grid that grows or shrinks refits its level, as Save does on the Mac. Play and Watch start at the maze's door, or its start flag. Hold the Gauntlet tool to go to the maze, move or swap it, or take it out.

The generators

The inspector’s Generators card (and the dice in the top bar). Roll a new course (⇧⌘L) lays the level anew with the game’s own generator, a new seed each press: the length, difficulty and saws its number has, no gauntlet, its notes kept; each press is one step in its history, so undo goes back a roll. Add a generated maze / Generate a new maze (⇧⌘G) makes a maze (MazeGen.h: floors joined by shafts, as the tomb is built, with saws, dips in the roof and steps at random) and keeps only one that passes the checklist (one way through, no traps, out with the slow meter, 15 near misses); with no maze yet it’s put in at 40% of the way as maze_N, else it takes the maze’s place (a step of the maze’s history). Its size: the length (80–260 cells) and 3, 5 or 7 floors. In the maze editor, Roll a new maze does the same.

Tool groups

Related tools share one button in the rail (the strip on an iPhone): Build (Walls, Kind, Barrier, Block), Hazards (every danger in the game’s registry: saw, wall laser, lava, and each zone’s new one), Edit (Columns, Select); the disco ball, good for her, keeps its own. A group’s button wears the tool it last had, with a wedge in its corner: a tap picks that tool; tapped again while it’s the tool, or held, its flyout opens beside the rail (over the strip) with each tool, its key and what it does. The last pick is kept between launches, and every key still picks its tool straight away (Z the laser, A lava). The eraser (under the rail) is a tool of its own, whichever tool is picked: what’s under the finger goes (a hazard or a pickup, a block, a barrier, the walls down to the street), dragged along as one step; picking anything else on the rail puts it away. The maze editor groups Columns and Rows as Lines (each row of its flyout with copy in and take out), and its cells in four: Terrain, Bouncers, Hazards, Doors (each cell’s row with its eraser button: erase with that cell).

Backups

The levels list’s Backup button (the drive). Back up all levels… saves one file (Cuckoo Editor backup <date>.json) wherever you pick in Files (iCloud Drive, On My iPad, a USB drive): every level and maze on the device as the game reads them, and their histories; the Mac’s pairing code isn’t in it. Keep it outside the app’s own folder: deleting the app deletes that folder. Restore from a backup… picks one and, after you confirm, puts each of its levels and mazes in place of the one here (the others here stay); any that differ from the Mac’s sync to it as changes.

Apple Pencil

Once it’s used, only the Pencil draws and fingers pan. A hovering Pencil shows where it would land; a double-tap switches to the eraser or the tool before (as its settings say); a Pencil Pro’s squeeze opens the tools in a ring at its tip.

Sync with the Mac

Run the server with --lan (python3 tools/level_editor/server.py --lan): it prints a pairing code and shows up on the same Wi-Fi as “Cuckoo Editor on your Mac”. Pair once (the levels list’s banner); after that the app syncs when it’s opened, when it’s back on Wi-Fi, or with the sync button. A maze's grid edited on the device goes to the Mac the same way (with its level, refitted), and one changed on both sides isn't merged: you pick, the two drawn with the cells that differ outlined. Each level comes together against the text both sides last agreed on: changed on one side, it goes to the other; changed on both, the game’s merge (LevelMerge.h: field by field, column by column, hazards as sets) puts the two together, and where it can’t it asks: keep mine, take the Mac’s, or keep mine as a new level. What the phone sends is saved as Save saves (checked, refitted, the game’s .inc files rebuilt); nothing is committed, nothing is written during a git merge, and a level taken out goes to build/editor-trash.

Changing the editor

For whoever extends the editor next: how it’s built, how to add to it, and the one rule.

Its parts

Recipes

Add a tool

  1. Its icon, key and label in tools.js; its tip in tips.js.
  2. What it does on the canvas in levels.js (or gauntlets.js): its pointerdown case, and an edit through commit(L, kind).
  3. Its words in the History: a kind case in describe.js.
  4. Its model in course.js / grid.js, tested in tests/*.test.mjs.
  5. Its section here, with a figure, and a changelog line.

Add a hazard

  1. The game first: its row in the hazards’ registry (src/core/hazards/<Name>.cpp, listed in Hazards.cpp: its section in the level file, its numbers and their ranges, its tool’s key, label, tip and colour, how the course lays it, its checks and egg cost), then its own play and drawing in the game. The level file, the checker and the iPhone and iPad editor take it from the registry.
  2. The course model (course.js: read, write, edit) and its drawing (courseview.js).
  3. A tool for it, its hover card (hazardAt in levels.js) and its tip.
  4. Its card in this wiki’s Pieces and hazards.

Add a check

  1. The test that enforces it, in the game’s tests.
  2. The checker reporting it in its JSON.
  3. An item in the view’s verdict(), so Ready to ship says what the test will.
  4. A row in Checks and the tests.

Its tests

ctest runs them with the game’s: level_editor (the server: serving, checking, saving, playing, what it turns away) and level_editor_js (in Node: the course and grid models, the history and its words, and this wiki, which must have a section for every tip).

The rule. Every change to the editor (a tool, hazard, check, panel, button, shortcut, file format) updates this wiki in the same change: its section, its figure, its tip in tips.js, and a line in the changelog. The level_editor_js test fails when a tip has no section here (id="tip-<key>"), so an undocumented feature blocks commits.
